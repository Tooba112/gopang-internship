About Temperature Scales

Temperature is a measure of the thermal energy of matter. Three main scales are used worldwide: Celsius, Fahrenheit, and Kelvin. Each scale was developed at different times and is used in different contexts today.

Celsius (°C)

The Celsius scale, developed by Anders Celsius in 1742, is the most widely used temperature scale worldwide. Water freezes at 0°C and boils at 100°C at standard atmospheric pressure. Pakistan, like most countries, uses Celsius for everyday weather reports and daily temperature measurements.

Fahrenheit (°F)

Developed by Daniel Gabriel Fahrenheit in 1724, the Fahrenheit scale sets the freezing point of water at 32°F and boiling at 212°F. It is primarily used in the United States for everyday temperature reporting.

Kelvin (K)

The Kelvin scale is the SI unit of temperature and is used in scientific applications. Zero Kelvin (absolute zero) represents the theoretical lowest possible temperature — the point at which all molecular motion ceases. Water freezes at 273.15 K and boils at 373.15 K.

Important Reference Points

  • Absolute zero: −273.15°C = −459.67°F = 0 K
  • Freezing point of water: 0°C = 32°F = 273.15 K
  • Boiling point of water: 100°C = 212°F = 373.15 K
  • Normal body temperature: 37°C = 98.6°F = 310.15 K
  • Room temperature: ~20–22°C = 68–72°F
